ContrastContrast

Difference is information.

Law IIIof VII

WhiteSpace

Silence is a material.

Space isn’t empty — it’s the frame that tells the eye where to rest. Crowd a thing and it shouts over everything else. Give it room and it finally gets heard.

Breathe.

36 things competing for you

This emptiness is intentional — 80vh of it.
Try it — 02

Leading & measure

The space inside text matters as much as the space around it. Same words, same size — only the line height, line length and tracking change.

White space is the most underrated material in design. It costs nothing, it never goes out of style, and it does the quiet work of grouping, separating and pacing — telling the reader what belongs together and when to pause. When a paragraph is cramped, the eye loses its place at the end of every line and has to hunt for the next one. Give the lines room to breathe and a comfortable length, around sixty characters, and reading stops feeling like work.

Law IVof VII

Color

It arrives before meaning.

Before a single word is read, color has already set the mood. Choose colors as a family, not as individuals — harmony is a relationship, measured in angles on a wheel.

Law Vof VII

Typography

Every typeface has a voice.

Type is how words get dressed for the occasion. Weight, width, size and spacing change what a sentence feels like long before anyone reads it. Move your pointer over the title — it’s one variable font, breathing.

Law VIof VII

Motion

Easing is emotion.

Nothing real starts or stops instantly. Easing gives movement weight and intent — the same distance in the same time can feel robotic, hesitant, confident or delighted. Poke the title.

Law VIIof VII

Balance

Felt long before it’s seen.

Every shape carries visual weight: size, darkness and distance from the center all add to it. Balance isn’t symmetry — it’s a truce. A small dark square far out can hold up a big bright circle close in.

Try it — 10

Level the beam

Drag the shapes onto the beam. Weight × distance from the pivot tips it one way or the other. Find a truce with at least one shape on each side.
